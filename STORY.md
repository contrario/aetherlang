# From Kitchen to Code

**My name is Ilias Stavrou. I am a chef. I have been a chef for 23 years.**

---

A year ago I was watching TikTok videos of people trying to sell ChatGPT prompts. They all said the same thing: *"Write this prompt and AI will show you how to make money."*

I took screenshots of those videos, pasted them into ChatGPT, and one prompt led to another. After endless hours inside chat windows I found myself with a collection of prompts — and answers to questions nobody had thought to ask yet.

I had read that developers were the future. That whoever could build apps and systems would have work and money. And I could see that I couldn't keep working in a kitchen until retirement at 68. So I decided to build my first application.

The first attempts were completely tragic. I understood nothing. I needed an entire conversation with an AI just to create a Git repository.

---

## The Road

The road was very hard.

Sleepless nights. No personal life. I got pneumonia last summer and I was still in front of the screen, fighting with the unknown, trying to make something work. At the same time I had to keep working as a chef. I have an 8-year-old child who needed my time — so I tried to divide everything. I also have my mother with chronic illnesses who I take care of.

When I had a moment, I tried to build. To learn. To read. To watch a video that explained the how and why of artificial intelligence. Most things I didn't manage to understand.

Most people, when I tried to explain what I was doing, smiled ironically. *"You, the cook — what do you know about all this?"*

---

## November 7, 2025

On November 6, 2025 I sent a message to an AI that started with this:

*"Μετά από πολλές δοκιμές και αποτυχίες τα κατάφερα και έφτασα μέχρι εδώ."*

After many attempts and failures I managed it and got this far.

Along with that message I attached a file called `PROJECT_HANDOVER_v4.0.md`. A handover document. A map of everything I had built. Version 3.9 of something called AKOUZO — a culinary AI platform with 7 modules: recipe engine, waste management, costing, inventory, translator, orchestrator. Built over months of nights I can't fully account for.

The next day I sent four words:

**"https://akouzo.omnimusmind.com ta katafera telika"**

*I finally managed it.*

The first time something I built was live on the internet. From my phone. Working.

I don't think I slept that night either. But for a different reason.

---

## What Came After

I still haven't learned to write code. But that's what AI is for — at least that's the part I chose to use.

After testing many LLMs I ended up with Claude. And slowly things started to take a different shape. Still with failures and endless sleepless nights — but each failure taught me one more step.

I built NeuroDoc — a Greek OCR platform for digitizing handwritten documents. I was charging €7 for 20 pages, writing posts in Greek Facebook groups for teachers and tutoring centers, wondering if anyone would actually pay.

Some did.

Then I started building agents. The culinary AI grew into an orchestration framework. A DSL. I called it AetherLang.

ChatGPT 4 gave me the initial concept. It could see me trying to build something inside those chat windows and it was as if it took pity on me. The first version was very simple. But I kept going.

---

## Where I Am Today

I don't know exactly how it happened.

One day I didn't have a GitHub repo. Today I have three servers on Hetzner running an agent ecosystem I built myself — **65 autonomous agents, 121 tools, 326 flows in production.**

The system runs a Sleep Cycle every night at 3am that consolidates memory across agents. It has a CONSTITUTION with cryptographic verification. A neuromorphic router. A genetic YAML engineer that rewrites itself. I don't fully understand every word I just wrote — but I built the things those words describe.

I have a LinkedIn profile where I write that I am a systems architect. When the AI first called me that, I laughed. *Me? A simple cook?* But eventually I stopped arguing with it.

I have a profile on Upwork. I have done an interview with an agency in England. A Greek tax advisor agent answers accountants on WhatsApp in Greek, every day, without me doing anything.

---

## What I Know

I don't know how to code. I don't have a computer science degree. I don't have investors or a team.

What I have is a promise I made to my child and to myself: that I will change our lives. I don't know exactly how. But I will.

The only thing I know for certain is this: every time I stopped analyzing whether I was allowed to do something and just did it, something happened. The first deployment. The first paying user. The Upwork profile. The interview.

If I had thought *"who am I to do this"* — I never would have done any of it.

---

**AetherLang is not a side project.**

It started as screenshots from TikTok videos about making money with AI prompts. It became something I can't fully describe yet.

But it runs. Every day. On its own.
